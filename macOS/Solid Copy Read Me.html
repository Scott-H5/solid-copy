Solid Copy

A ham radio logging app for the Mac, designed from the start for VoiceOver and the keyboard.

"Solid copy" is ham shorthand for "I received everything."

Status

Solid Copy is an everyday logger, used on the air with VoiceOver. It:

  • logs a contact from the keyboard alone, starting in the Callsign field
  • fills in name, QTH and grid from QRZ (or callook.info)
  • reads frequency, band, mode and power from the radio, through its own built-in driver for Kenwood and Elecraft radios, or hamlib's rigctld for others (tested with the Kenwood TS-590SG and Elecraft KX3)
  • uploads to LoTW, QRZ Logbook, Club Log and eQSL, automatically if you like, and brings LoTW and QRZ confirmations back
  • warns about dupes, and says each contact's country and whether it's a new one, band or mode
  • runs contests (ARRL Field Day first), with serial numbers and CW messages through a WinKeyer
  • tracks QSLs and parks, shows spots, and makes an electronic QSL card you can send by email
  • keeps separate logs, imports ADIF, ADX and log files in the SQL format (.sql), exports ADIF, ADX and CSV, and backs up to a folder you choose

See docs/PLAN.md for where it's headed.

Installing

Download the zip from the latest release at https://github.com/Scott-H5/solid-copy/releases and open it. You get a folder with two things in it: Solid Copy.app and this Read Me (Solid Copy Read Me.html). Drag Solid Copy.app to your Applications folder, then open it. It's signed and notarized, so macOS opens it without warnings. It needs macOS 14 (Sonoma) or later.

This Read Me is also inside the app: Help > Solid Copy Help (Command-?) opens it in your web browser, with no internet needed, so it's there in the field too. Help > Solid Copy on GitHub opens the project's page, with the latest version. In the browser, VO-Command-H moves from heading to heading.

Testing it

Thank you for trying Solid Copy. What's most useful to hear about is how it feels to use:

  • Is it intuitive? Could you find your way around without help, and did things work the way you expected?
  • Is it easy to navigate? With VoiceOver and the keyboard: the logging form, the log, the menus and Settings.
  • Does the layout make logging efficient? Can you log a contact quickly, without extra steps?
  • Is saving, changing or editing contacts clear? Was anything about logging a contact, editing one, or deleting one confusing?

If something goes wrong, please say:

  • which radio you're using
  • your version of macOS (Apple menu > About This Mac)
  • what kind of Mac it is (for example, a MacBook Air with an M2)

Reply to the email that invited you, or open an issue at https://github.com/Scott-H5/solid-copy/issues.

First setup

The first time Solid Copy opens, with no callsign set, a short sheet asks for three things:

  • Callsign: yours, such as W1AW. Every contact you log carries it, and the uploads (LoTW, QRZ, Club Log, eQSL) need it.
  • Name: optional. It's used by the CW messages' {MYNAME}.
  • Grid square: optional, 4 or 6 characters, such as FN31 or FN31pr. It's used for distance and bearing, and for LoTW.

Save keeps them. Skip leaves them empty, and then the sheet doesn't come back; you can fill them in later under Settings > Profiles (callsign and grid) and Settings > CW (name). Set Up Station… in Settings > Profiles shows this sheet again at any time. A profile with a callsign already never shows the sheet on its own.

Then, to log a contact: type the callsign in the Callsign field and press Tab through the fields, then Return to log it. The log keeps every contact as you go.

The log's columns start in the order Call, Name, Date, Time, Band, Frequency, Mode, RST Sent, RST Received, Country, QSO notes. Every other field is in the details panel under the log.

QSO notes is for your own use. It is never uploaded to LoTW, QRZ Logbook, Club Log or eQSL, and it is left out of exports unless you tick Include QSO notes. The Comment field is different: it goes out with the contact, so use it only for what you want the other station and the services to see.

Operating profiles

A profile, such as Home or Portable, is a logging form plus your station's details (callsign, grid, state, county, radio, park, summit…), which are added to every contact logged with it. Switch with the Profile menu or Option-Command-1, 2, 3…; the window title says which is in use. Settings > Profiles (Command-comma, then Command-2) adds, renames and deletes profiles and holds each one's station details. Portable starts with Band, Mode, Frequency and Power on the form, for when the radio or the internet isn't there, with Power starting at 5 watts.

New Profile is a menu: a copy of the profile in use, or a fresh one from a template: Home, Portable, POTA activation (their park, for park to park), SOTA activation (their summit), Field Day (their class and section) or State QSO Party (their county or state). A template sets the form; your station comes from the profile in use, without its park or summit. Change anything afterwards in Logging Form.

Parks

Park fields search POTA's list of every park, kept on your Mac, so they work in a park with no internet: POTA park (theirs) on the logging form, and My POTA park in Settings > Profiles. Type a reference ("US-13"), part of a name ("big poc") or a state ("pennsylvania state", or "PA"). After a pause you hear how many parks match. Down and Up Arrow go through them, each said in full ("US-1333, Big Pocono State Park, Pennsylvania. 1 of 3"). Leaving the field with only one match takes it. Return won't log a contact whose park is still a search: it says how many match. For a two-fer, put a comma between parks ("US-1333,US-1334").

The list (about 106,000 parks, 10 MB) is fetched the first time you use a park field, and again when it's a month old. Settings > Lookup > Get the Park List Now fetches it whenever you like.

RST sent and RST received take only digits, with a plus or minus in front: 59, 599, or -12 for FT8. Anything else beeps and isn't entered.

A contest profile (one with Contest on its form) or a park activation (one with your park) warns of a dupe when you leave Callsign: "Dupe: W1AW worked on 20 meters CW at 14:32 UTC", said first, with the dupe sound, and shown on the lookup line. You can still log it.

Spots

Window > Spots (Command-1) lists who's on the air, newest first, one line each, from POTA ("W1AW, 14.062 CW, US-1333 Big Pocono State Park, Pennsylvania, 3 minutes ago, worked today") and, once you turn it on in Settings > Spots, a DX cluster ("VP8XYZ, 14.025 CW, Falkland Islands, needed: new country, 2 minutes ago. CQ CQ up 2, spotted by K1ABC"). It updates while the window is open (POTA each minute, the cluster every 10 seconds at most); a new list never moves you or talks.

Key What it does
Up, Down Move between spots
Return Use the spot: tunes the radio to it (frequency and mode), puts its call and park in the logging form, and goes there. Add the reports and press Return to log
Escape Back to the logging window (Spots stays open)
Tab To the filters: Source (POTA, DX cluster), Band (all bands, your radio's current band, or any one band), Mode (CW, phone, data), Hide spots worked today, DX: needed only, and DX: heard near me

"Worked today" follows POTA's rule: that park, on that band and mode, this UTC day. Command-backtick moves between Spots, the logging window and Settings.

DX cluster. A cluster node passes on spots from hams everywhere, thousands an hour. Turn it on in Settings > Spots and choose a node: VE7CC (dxc.ve7cc.net, port 23) to start with, W3LPL, K3LR, or any other by its address and port. Solid Copy logs in with the callsign in the profile in use, only while the Spots window is open, and reconnects if it drops. Each DX spot says its country (from cty.dat, fetched from country-files.com and refreshed monthly) and whether you need it: a new country, or a new band or mode for one, worked out from your log. A spot's mode is worked out from its comment or where it is on the band. DX: needed only leaves just those. Many nodes (VE7CC among them) send only spots typed by people unless asked: Include skimmer spots adds CW stations heard by the Reverse Beacon Network's receivers, and Include FT8 and FT4 spots adds those. Both make many more spots, so they start off. A station spotted by several skimmers says so ("heard by 12": the more, the stronger and wider it's heard). DX: heard near me keeps only spots from spotters on your own continent (from your callsign in the profile in use), the ones you're likely to hear too. SOTA spots come from the DX cluster: a cluster spot whose comment names a summit ("W4C/CM-001") is listed as a SOTA spot, with its summit, and the Source filter can show just those. Return on one fills in their summit (SOTA summit). Only spots posted to the cluster are seen, so it's most SOTA activations, not all: SOTA's own API doesn't allow AI-assisted software without SOTA's approval (its terms, at api-db2.sota.org.uk/docs). SOTA's database takes ADIF uploads, so File > Export ADIF makes your SOTA log (it has MY_SOTA_REF and SOTA_REF).

Settings

Settings (Command-comma) is laid out like System Settings: a list of sections down the left (Lookup, Profiles, Logging Form, Log Columns, Radio, Speech, Spots, Uploads, Clubs, Backups) and the chosen section on the right, scrolling if it's tall. Command-1 to Command-9 go straight to a section. In the list, Up and Down choose a section and Return or Right Arrow go into it; Tab goes round the section and back to the list. Escape or Command-W closes Settings.

Speech

Solid Copy talks through VoiceOver, so its messages reach a braille display too, and nothing is said when VoiceOver is off. Three kinds of message come often and can talk over a contact you're copying, so Settings > Speech lets you choose how much each says:

Message Choices
Callsign lookups Say everything, name only, sound only, or nothing
Logging a contact Say everything, just "Logged", or sound only
Radio changes you make Say everything, or sound only
Radio lost or back Its tone and the words, or the tone only

Errors and warnings are always said in full. The Speech menu brings back what you turned down, whenever you want it:

Key What it does
Option-Command-period Quiet Mode: lookups, logging and radio changes down to sounds, until you turn it off (for a pile-up, say)
Option-Command-N Say the name and place for the call in the form (looks it up if it hasn't been)
Option-Command-L Say the last contact logged
Command-R (Radio menu) Say what the radio is doing

Most sounds are ones every Mac has: Glass for logged, Pop for a lookup found, Bottle for not found, Tink for a radio change, Basso for a problem. The moments that must stand out have Solid Copy's own sounds, made so they sound like nothing else on the Mac, and nothing like a CW signal or sidetone:

  • A dupe: two short low buzzes.
  • Needed: two quick bells going up, for a call you type that's a new country, band or mode, and for a needed DX spot as it comes in.
  • The radio: two falling notes when it's lost, two rising when it's back.

Choose the dupe and needed sounds in Settings > Speech, under Sounds (Solid Copy's others, any Mac sound, or none; choosing one plays it). Needed sound for DX spots is New countries only to start with (a young log needs nearly every band and mode), New country, band or mode, or No sound. A spot plays it once per call and band, and at most every 30 seconds, while the Spots window is getting spots. These sounds play whatever the speech levels say, Quiet Mode too.

Logging a contact with no band (most often because the radio dropped without your noticing) gives the warning sound and "No band": type the band or frequency, or press Return again to log it anyway. A frequency typed without a band fills the band in by itself.

The radio

Solid Copy reads frequency, band, mode and power from the radio. For Kenwood and Elecraft radios (TS-590S, TS-590SG, K3, KX3, KX2) it uses its own built-in driver, which talks to the radio's USB port directly, so nothing else needs installing. Any other radio works through hamlib's rigctld, which you run yourself (see "Hamlib NET rigctl" below).

Add your radios in Settings > Radio (Command-comma, Command-5): New Radio, then its connection, manufacturer, model, USB port and speed (the TS-590SG uses 115200, the KX3 38400). One radio is in use at a time, whatever the profile.

  • Band scan (Radio > Band Scan, Control-Command-C) steps up the band a kilohertz at a time, pausing at each, and turns back at the top. Press it again to stop. It says when it starts and stops, not at each step.

  • Switch radios in the middle of a session: Control-Command-1, 2… (the Radio menu lists them), or the radio pop-up under the clocks.

  • The radio pop-up's label is the radio and what it's doing: "Kenwood TS-590SG: 7.025241 MHz, CW, 40m, 5 W, menu button". Its menu has your radios, No Radio, and Radio Settings.

  • The form's radio fields fill in as you tune. Type in one and yours is kept until the form is cleared. Fields not on the form are still logged from the radio.

  • Command-R (Radio > Radio Status) says frequency, mode, filter, band and power, or what's wrong. Option-Command-R (Radio Details) says the line at the bottom of the window: keyer speed, RIT, XIT, split, AGC, preamp and attenuator (whichever the radio reports).

The tuning control is a slider to VoiceOver ("Tuning, 7025.30 kilohertz"); each change is spoken, including turns of the radio's own knob while you're there. Each command says what the radio actually did ("CW, 500 hertz", "Keyer 22 words per minute", "Preamp 12 dB"), or that the radio can't ("Elecraft KX3 didn't change its AGC"). If VoiceOver's Quick Nav is on, it takes the arrow keys; VO-Up and VO-Down tune instead.

  • Changes are spoken once, naming the radio: "Kenwood TS-590SG connected…", "Elecraft KX3 off or unplugged", "… not answering".
  • Solid Copy never gives up: switch the radio on before or after it, off and on again, or unplug and replug it, and it reconnects by itself. Radio > Reconnect Radio starts afresh if you ever want to.
  • A missed answer or two isn't called a lost radio: Solid Copy says "lost" only after 4 seconds without one. Restarting VoiceOver with a USB braille display plugged in can make the radio miss a second's read, which before version 1.1.2 was announced as lost, then back.
  • Programs such as iDigi and WSJT-X can use the radio through Solid Copy while you log: see "Sharing the radio with other programs" below. The built-in driver holds the radio's USB port itself, so a rigctld you run can't open that radio at the same time.

Hamlib NET rigctl: a rigctld you run yourself

Settings > Radio > Connect through has Hamlib NET rigctl for a rigctld you run yourself, from hamlib, on this Mac or another one. Set its IP address (127.0.0.1 is this Mac) and Network port (4532 is rigctld's usual port). Solid Copy then uses that rigctld and nothing else.

To get hamlib, install it with Homebrew (see https://brew.sh for installing Homebrew):

brew install hamlib

Then start rigctld for your radio. These are the TS-590SG (model 2037, 115200 baud) and the KX3 (model 2045, 38400 baud); use your radio's model number and the port from Settings > Radio:

rigctld -m 2037 -r /dev/cu.usbserial-XXXX -s 115200 -t 4532

Leave that running while you log. Hamlib's own instructions, and its full list of radios, are at https://hamlib.github.io/.

To change from the built-in driver to a rigctld you run without restarting anything: choose Hamlib NET rigctl in Settings > Radio first (that lets go of the radio's port), then start rigctld. To go back, quit rigctld (Control-C) first, then choose the built-in driver.

Sharing the radio with other programs

Settings > Radio > Share the radio with other programs lets programs that can use Hamlib NET rigctl, such as iDigi and WSJT-X, use the radio through Solid Copy while you keep logging, with no rigctld to run and no switching. Solid Copy answers them the way hamlib's rigctld would.

In the other program, choose the radio Hamlib NET rigctl, with the network server 127.0.0.1 and port 4532. That's all.

  • It works while the radio in use connects through the built-in driver. (With Hamlib NET rigctl, the rigctld you run shares the radio itself.)
  • Only programs on this Mac can connect; nothing on your network can.
  • They can tune the radio, change its mode and VFO, and turn split on and off.
  • Let them transmit (under it, off until you turn it on) lets them key the radio too (PTT). On the TS-590 they transmit from its data input, the USB audio they play; set the program's PTT method to CAT. Turning it on or off works at once, even for programs already connected. Try it into a dummy load first.
  • Solid Copy keeps the radio from being left on the air. It goes back to receive if the program that keyed it goes (quits, crashes, loses its connection), if sharing or Let them transmit is turned off, if you choose another radio, when Solid Copy quits, and after 5 minutes of one transmission. Each of those is said.
  • One program transmits at a time. A program's first transmission is said ("A program is transmitting through Solid Copy"); later ones aren't, since FT8 keys every 15 seconds. A program saying "receive" never cuts off you transmitting with your own microphone.
  • If another program, such as a rigctld, already has port 4532, Solid Copy says so and doesn't share the radio.
  • Solid Copy says when a program connects ("A program connected to the radio through Solid Copy") and when it goes. Window > Activity lists those, and when sharing starts and stops.

Choosing the form and the columns

Settings > Logging Form (Command-comma, then Command-3) lists every field you can put on the profile in use's form after Callsign; the Starting value field below it sets what the selected field starts as. Settings > Log Columns (Command-4) lists the log's columns. In either list, Space shows or hides the selected item, which stays where it is, and Option-Command-Up and Down move it past the next item in use. QSL statuses and dates (cards, LoTW, eQSL, QRZ Logbook) aren't offered for the form, since nobody knows them while logging: the QSL menu, syncing and Edit Contact set them. On the form, a field can be on screen without being a Tab stop: Make Tab Stop and Remove Tab Stop are in the Actions rotor (VO-Command-Space) and the shortcut menu. Name starts as not a Tab stop, because lookups fill it in. Changes show at once. Drag a column heading to change the order; Solid Copy remembers it.

Building

You need Xcode and XcodeGen (brew install xcodegen), and your Apple Developer team ID in a file named team.txt in this folder. From the folder you cloned (or downloaded) Solid Copy into:

bash build.sh install

That builds the app, quits Solid Copy if it's open, and installs it as /Applications/Solid Copy.app. It doesn't open it: open it yourself from the Dock or Spotlight, so it comes up in front and never takes focus while you're typing somewhere else.

Dupes, countries and "needed"

When you leave Callsign:

  • Dupes. The same call on the same band and mode today (CW, phone and data counted as one mode each) gets a warning, with the warning sound, before the lookup: "Dupe: W 1 A W already worked today on 20 meters CW at 14:32 UTC." You can still log it. In a contest log the contest's rule is used instead, and on a park activation the park's.
  • Country and needed. The call's country (DXCC entity) comes from cty.dat, the country list nearly every logger uses (fetched monthly). If you need it, you hear it with the lookup: "needed: new country", "new band" or "new mode", counted from the log that's open. The country is said on its own when the lookup gives no place, and it goes in the contact's Country when the lookup doesn't fill it.

Callsign lookups

Open Solid Copy > Settings… (Command-comma), enter your QRZ.com username and password, and press Save and Test. The password is kept in the Keychain. A free QRZ account gives only a few fields (mostly the name); a paid XML subscription gives everything. callook.info, free and with no account, can fill in US calls. Settings also has your own callsign and grid square, added to each new contact.

When you leave the Callsign field, the call is looked up. The result is shown under the form and spoken once, for example "W 1 A W, Hiram, Newington Connecticut, 282 miles north-east, worked 3 times before." Focus doesn't move.

Distance and bearing come from the grid square in your profile (My grid square, Settings > Profiles) to the station's position from the lookup (its latitude and longitude, or its grid). The line also gives the bearing in degrees, for pointing an antenna ("282 miles, 53 degrees north-east"). Miles or kilometres follows your Mac's region setting. Each contact keeps the distance in kilometres (ADIF's DISTANCE).

Club numbers

SKCC, FISTS, LICW and CWops numbers each have a field, and so do up to five clubs of your own (Settings > Clubs: name them, such as "NAQCC number"). Put the ones you use on the form in Settings > Logging Form.

When you leave Callsign, empty club fields are filled in, and the numbers are said with the lookup ("..., SKCC 12345 Tribune, worked twice before"):

  • SKCC from SKCC's own member list. Solid Copy keeps a copy (about 2 MB, fetched again each week; Settings > Clubs has Fetch SKCC List Now). It's the authority, so a member's newest award letter (C Centurion, T Tribune, S Senator) is the one logged.
  • Everything else from your log: the last contact with the same call that has the number. FISTS and LICW publish no member lists, so this is how they're filled: log a regular's number once, and it's there next time.

Checking a copied number. An SKCC number you type is checked against the call when you leave the field (or leave Callsign, if you typed the number first). If it belongs to someone else, you hear the warning sound and whose number it is: "SKCC 12345 is W 1 A B C. W 1 A B D isn't in SKCC's list." One of the two was miscopied, and a lookup alone can't tell you, because a miscopied call is often a real call. You can still log it as it is; SKCC's list may be a few days behind.

QSLs

A QSL is the other station's confirmation of a contact: a paper card, or LoTW, eQSL or QRZ Logbook. The details panel (Tab in the log) says it in one line, right after the time: "QSL: Confirmed: card 17 December 2025, LoTW 23 April 2022. Uploaded to QRZ Logbook." Each QSL field below it is in words ("Card received: Yes", not "Y").

The QSL menu works on the contacts selected in the log (and the log's shortcut menu, VO-Shift-M, has the same marks):

  • Card Requested: they asked you for a card.
  • Card Sent (Shift-Command-S): dated today. A request becomes sent.
  • Card Received (Shift-Command-R): dated today.
  • Show Confirmed, Show Not Confirmed, Show Card Requests: narrow the log. Escape shows the whole log again.

A mark on contacts that all have it already takes it off, and Command-Z undoes any of them. Select several contacts (Shift-Up, Shift-Down) to mark them all at once.

Importing a log file in the SQL format keeps cards received ("CardC"), LoTW and QRZ Logbook confirmations, and QRZ Logbook uploads.

Scanned cards

QSL > Attach Cards from a Folder… takes a folder of scanned cards (PDFs or pictures), each named by callsign ("W1AW.pdf"; "W1AW 2.pdf" for a second one), and attaches each to its contact. A call with several contacts gets the card only if just one of them is already marked card received; otherwise the summary lists it, so you can choose the contact yourself. Cards are copied into a QSL Cards folder beside the log, so the folder you chose can be moved or deleted afterwards, and each card counts as received. The text of a searchable scan is kept with the contact, so Command-F finds words on a card ("Pottawatomie"). Edit > Undo takes the whole lot back off.

For the contact selected in the log:

  • Open Card (Shift-Command-O): opens it in Preview, where VoiceOver reads the scan's text. Also in the log's shortcut menu.
  • Attach Card…: one card, for a contact the folder couldn't match.
  • Remove Card: takes it off (still counted as received).

What the other operator wrote on their card goes in Their card's message, in Edit Contact (Return on a contact).

Your electronic QSL card

QSL > Send QSL Card (Shift-Command-K, also in the log's shortcut menu) makes your QSL card for the selected contact. It's a PDF the size of a paper card (5.5 by 3.5 inches), with real text VoiceOver reads in Preview, and it prints. It's saved in a Sent Cards folder beside the log and attached to a new Mail message, addressed to them if the log has their email. The message also holds the card's text, for anyone whose mail can't show the PDF. Check it and send it yourself. Once Mail has the card, the contact is marked Card sent, by electronic means, dated today; Edit > Undo takes the mark back. A contact already marked sent (a paper card) keeps its mark.

Set the card up in Settings > QSL Card (or QSL > QSL Card Settings…):

  • Fields on the card: which, and in what order, in the same kind of list as Logging Form (Space shows or hides; Option-Command-Up and Down move). Yours, such as your grid square, go at the top with your details; the rest describe the contact, read as one paragraph: "Date: 14 November 2026. Time: 14:32 UTC. Band: 40m. ..."
  • Your name, Your town or QTH, Another line: up to three lines under your call, such as your club numbers.
  • Message, when the contact has none: the contact's QSL message is used if it has one.
  • Picture: one picture behind the text (a straight key, your shack), kept faint so the text stays clear, and What the picture shows, a description that's the picture's alternative text in the PDF and the card's last line. No picture of your own? Use the J-38 Picture puts Solid Copy's photo of a J-38 straight key there, described for you. It's public domain (photo by Lou Sander; see docs/CREDITS.md), so cards need no credit line.
  • Show Sample Card: a card for a made-up contact, opened in Preview, so you can hear and see the result without sending anything.

The card is one column, read top to bottom: your call, your lines, "To Hiram, W1AW", the contact, the message, "73". If the fields you choose don't fit, the text gets smaller, then the card gets taller; nothing is ever cut off.

LoTW

QSL > Sync with LoTW (Shift-Command-L) uploads the contacts LoTW doesn't have yet, then gets your new confirmations, saying each step as it starts and ending with a summary. Set it up in Settings > Uploads, under Logbook of the World:

  • LoTW username and password: your LoTW website login, for confirmations. The password is kept in the Keychain.
  • TQSL station location: the name of the location in TQSL to sign with, exactly as TQSL shows it ("Anytown, N0CALL").
  • Upload contacts from: a day like 2020-01-01, or empty for every contact.
  • Upload automatically: see Automatic uploads.
  • Choose Folder for TQSL…: a folder (a new one in Documents is fine) where Solid Copy leaves the contacts for TQSL and TQSL leaves its log. Solid Copy is sandboxed, so it needs a folder you chose.
  • Install TQSL Script…: once. A Save dialog opens in the one folder macOS lets a sandboxed app run scripts from; press Save. The script (two lines) only starts TQSL with what Solid Copy passes it. Delete it from that folder to stop.

Uploads need TQSL (the ARRL's TrustedQSL) installed with your callsign certificate. TQSL signs and sends; it skips anything LoTW already has, so nothing is sent twice. If your certificate has a passphrase, TQSL asks for it in its own window; if TQSL can't unlock the certificate, the summary says so and nothing is marked uploaded. Contacts TQSL won't sign (a band that isn't a band, say) are left unmarked, with TQSL's reason. Confirmations are matched to contacts by call and band, within 30 minutes; any that match nothing in your log are listed.

QRZ Logbook

QSL > Sync with QRZ Logbook (Shift-Command-U) sends the contacts your QRZ logbook doesn't have yet, one at a time, and marks each one QRZ has as uploaded (Uploaded to QRZ Logbook, with the day). Then it gets the contacts QRZ says are confirmed and marks them in your log (QRZ Logbook confirmed, with the day), matched as LoTW's are: the same call and band, within 30 minutes. Any that match nothing in this log are listed. It needs a QRZ subscription. Set it up in Settings > Uploads, under QRZ Logbook. QSL > QRZ Logbook API Key… goes straight to the key field (and Sync with QRZ Logbook takes you there itself if no key is saved yet); paste the key and press Return. QSL > Upload Settings… opens the whole Uploads section.

  • API key: your logbook's key. On qrz.com, open your logbook, then its Settings; copy the key and paste it here. It's kept in the Keychain.
  • Upload contacts from and Upload automatically, as for LoTW.

A contact QRZ already has counts as uploaded. One QRZ refuses (a date outside the logbook's, or a station callsign that isn't the logbook's) is listed with QRZ's reason and left unmarked. If the internet is down, or the key isn't allowed, it stops and says so. Notes and card scans aren't sent.

Club Log

QSL > Upload to Club Log sends the contacts Club Log doesn't have yet, about one a second (Club Log allows 60 a minute), and marks each one it has (Uploaded to Club Log, with the day). Set it up in Settings > Uploads, under Club Log; QSL > Club Log Token… goes straight to the token field.

  • Token: on clublog.org, open Settings, then the API tab. Give the token a label (Solid Copy), leave Test mode unticked, and choose Create token. Copy it at once (Club Log shows it only once), paste it here, and press Return. It's kept in the Keychain.
  • Upload contacts from and Upload automatically, as for LoTW.

Each contact goes under its own My callsign (or your profile's, if it has none), which must be on the token's Club Log account. A contact Club Log refuses is listed with Club Log's reason. A token Club Log doesn't accept, or a callsign not on its account, stops the upload at once: Club Log can block your internet address for a while if a program keeps sending a failing request. The message names the callsign that was sent, so you can tell whether My callsign is wrong or Club Log's account is missing it (on clublog.org, the account's callsigns are under Settings).

eQSL

QSL > Upload to eQSL sends the contacts eQSL doesn't have yet, one a second, and marks each one it has (Uploaded to eQSL, with the day). Your card's message is the contact's QSL message. Set it up in Settings > Uploads, under eQSL; QSL > eQSL Login… goes straight there.

  • Username and Password: your eQSL login (the username is usually your callsign). The password is kept in the Keychain.
  • QTH Nickname: only if you have more than one eQSL account under the same callsign. Type the nickname of the one to use, as your eQSL profile names it.
  • Upload contacts from and Upload automatically, as for LoTW.

A contact eQSL refuses (a mode it doesn't take, no band, a date your account doesn't cover) is listed in plain words. A wrong login stops the upload.

Uploading just some contacts

In the log, the shortcut menu (VO-Shift-M, or Control-click) has Upload to LoTW, Upload to QRZ Logbook, Upload to Club Log and Upload to eQSL: just the selected contacts, now, even ones already sent (you chose them). It says how it went.

Reading or copying an upload message again

Every message window (an upload, an import, an export) has a Copy button after OK: Tab to it and press Space, and the whole message is on the clipboard, ready to paste into an email or a note.

An automatic upload, or one from the log's shortcut menu, only speaks its message. QSL > Show Last Upload Message shows the last upload or sync message again in a window you can read word by word, with Copy.

Automatic uploads

Each service's Upload automatically (Settings > Uploads) is one of:

  • By hand (the starting choice): only when you ask.
  • Soon after each contact: half a minute after you log it, so the lookup has filled it in and a quick correction goes with it.
  • Five minutes after the last contact: a run of contacts goes up together, with any corrections. Best for LoTW, which can't change a contact once it has it.

Only contacts you log on the form while Solid Copy is open go up this way; older ones and imports wait for Sync or Upload. When it works you hear nothing, so logging isn't interrupted. A problem is said once, with the warning sound. Without internet, the contacts wait and go with the next contact, or ten minutes later. Quitting while contacts wait is fine: they go half a minute after you next open Solid Copy.

CW through a WinKeyer

Solid Copy can send CW through a K1EL WinKeyer (tested with a WinKeyer Mini). Set it up in Settings > CW (the last section): the WinKeyer's port, the speed, your name, and the eight messages.

  • CW > Connect WinKeyer opens it. Only one app can have the WinKeyer at a time, so quit any other app using it first; if one has it, you hear so. Solid Copy never connects by itself, and lets go when it quits (the WinKeyer goes back to its own settings).
  • F1 to F8 send the messages. The CW menu lists each one, so reading the menu reminds you what each key sends.
  • Escape stops CW that's being sent. When nothing is sending, Escape clears the form as always.
  • Control-Command-= and - (Keyer Faster and Slower) change the WinKeyer's speed while it's connected, instead of the radio's keyer.

Each profile has its own messages: Settings > CW edits the ones for the profile in use. A profile made from the Field Day template starts with Field Day's (F1 CQ FD, F2 their call and your class and section, F3 TU and your call, and so on).

Messages can use fill-ins: {MYCALL}, {MYNAME}, {MYSTATE} are yours; {NR} is your serial number for this contact (see Serial numbers); {SENT} is your exchange, from Exchange sent on the form (Field Day's class and section, "1D MDC"); {CALL}, {NAME}, {STATE}, {SKCC} are theirs, from the form (or the lookup and SKCC's list); {RST} is the report you're sending, or 599. One with nothing to fill it is left out. F1 starts as CQ, F2 as an exchange (their call, RST, your name and state), F3 as TU and your call; change any of them. (SKCC's own events require a mechanical key, so keyer messages aren't for those.)

Sending CW keys your radio. Try new messages with the WinKeyer unplugged from the radio, or into a dummy load.

Contests

Contests are plain text files in Solid Copy's Contests folder (File > Contests > Show Contest Files opens it, with a README explaining the format). A change to a file, or a new file, works at once, with no new version of Solid Copy. ARRL Field Day's file is there to start:

name: ARRL Field Day
id: ARRL-FIELD-DAY
exchange: class and section
serial: no
dupes: band and mode
messages: CQ FD {MYCALL} {MYCALL} FD | {CALL} {SENT} | TU {MYCALL} FD | ...
submit: dupe sheet

Only name and id are needed. exchange lists what they send (name, state, county, rst, zone, grid, skcc, member and others, or any field's ADIF name); serial: yes adds Serial sent and received; dupes is once, band, or band and mode (modes grouped as CW, phone and digital); messages are the CW messages, split by |; submit: dupe sheet offers Field Day's dupe sheet in File > Export….

  • File > New Log > New Contest Log… offers each contest file, and "Other: use a profile" for a contest with no file. A contest log comes with a profile built from the file (its form and messages), brings it back whenever it's opened, and warns of dupes by the contest's rule over the whole log.
  • File > Contests > Save Profile as Contest… writes the profile in use as a contest file: a contest set up by hand, kept or shared.
  • File > Export…, Dupe sheet lists the calls worked by band and mode, headed with the contest and your call: what ARRL asks for with a Field Day entry. (ARRL treats a Cabrillo log as optional for Field Day and publishes no Field Day layout, so there's none yet.)

Serial numbers

Put Serial sent on a profile's form (Settings > Logging Form) and it counts itself: a fresh form shows the next number, 1 more than the highest logged in this log for the same contest (the form's Contest). Each log and contest has its own count, and it stays right after a deleted contact or a correction. Type over it to change it. Logging says the number ("Logged W 1 A W, number 12"; "Logged, 12" when logging speech is brief). Put Serial received on the form for theirs. CW messages can send the number as {NR}; it's logged as Serial sent even if that field isn't on the form. None of this needs the keyer.

Logs

The File menu keeps everything about logs together at the top: New Log (a submenu: New Log…, New Contest Log…, New Test Log…), Open Log (a submenu of every log), Delete This Log… and Show Log in Finder. Then Import…, Export… and Back Up Now; then Contests (a submenu: Show Contest Files, Save Profile as Contest…); then Save Contact and Close Window.

Solid Copy starts with one log, the main log. You can make more:

  • File > New Log > New Log… (Command-N): a log of its own, for a special event or a contest kept apart from everyday contacts. It uploads like the main log.
  • File > New Log > New Contest Log…: a log for one contest (ARRL Field Day for now), with that contest's profile: its form and CW messages. If you have no profile for it yet, one is made from the contest's template. Opening the log always brings that profile back.
  • File > New Log > New Test Log… (Shift-Command-N): for trying layouts, simulating a contest and so on. Nothing in a test log is ever uploaded, by hand or automatically, and its backups stay on this Mac.
  • File > Open Log: every log, the open one ticked. Choosing one switches to it and says which log is open and how many contacts it has. The window title always names the open log ("Solid Copy, Main log, Home").
  • File > Delete This Log… (any log but the main one): after asking, with how many contacts it has, moves the log, its backups and cards to the Trash and opens the main log. Anything already uploaded stays on LoTW and QRZ. The main log can't be deleted from the menu, on purpose: it's your real logbook. To empty it, select all in its log (Command-A) and press Delete (Command-Z undoes it). To remove it altogether, quit Solid Copy, use File > Show Log in Finder first to find its folder, and move Logbook.sqlite to the Trash (leave the Logs folder: it holds your other logs); the next launch starts an empty main log. Backups, beside the log and in your backup folder, are kept.

Copying contacts between logs: select contacts in the log (Shift-Up and Shift-Down select several), press Command-C, open the other log, move into its log and press Command-V. Every field comes across, Notes too; contacts already in that log are skipped, and Command-Z takes the paste back out. A scanned card stays with the log it was attached in. The copy is ordinary ADIF text, so ADIF copied from elsewhere pastes too. Command-V with contacts on the clipboard pastes them into the open log from the form too (Callsign or any field), leaving the field as it was, so there's no need to move into the log, even an empty one.

Each log remembers the profile last used with it, and opening the log brings that profile back, with its form and CW messages: a "Field Day 2027" log comes back with your Field Day profile, and the main log with Home. You hear the profile with the log's name.

Each log has its own contacts, backups and scanned cards. Profiles, radios, lookups and your logins are shared by all of them. Switching needs an empty form, so a contact you're typing is never lost: log it or press Escape first. Solid Copy opens the log you had open when you quit.

Backups

Solid Copy backs up the log each time it opens, and before imports, keeping the newest 20 beside the log (File > Show Log in Finder). Those go if the Mac or its drive does, so choose a second folder in Settings > Backups (the last section in the sidebar): Choose Backup Folder… opens the usual Open dialog, where iCloud Drive is in the sidebar and New Folder makes one.

A copy then goes there when Solid Copy opens; while it's open, on the schedule in Settings > Backups (Every 4 hours to start, or every hour, once a day, or never); when it quits; and with File > Back Up Now. The schedule and quitting only back up if the log changed. They also back up beside the log when no folder is chosen. The newest 20 are kept there as "Solid Copy log 2026-11-16 143205Z.sqlite", with your scanned cards in a QSL Cards folder. You hear nothing when it works; a problem is said with the warning sound (one at quit is said the next time you open Solid Copy).

To restore: quit Solid Copy, then copy a backup over Logbook.sqlite in the folder File > Show Log in Finder opens, renaming it Logbook.sqlite.

Importing and exporting

File > Import… (Shift-Command-I) reads ADIF files (.adi, .adif), ADX files (.adx, ADIF written as XML) and log files in the SQL format (.sql). It works out which from the file itself. The file is only read, never changed. Solid Copy backs up its own log first, skips contacts it already has, and Edit > Undo takes the import back out.

File > Export… (Shift-Command-E) saves the whole log as ADIF, as ADX or as a CSV spreadsheet. ADIF and ADX carry exactly the same fields; use whichever the other program or service asks for. Notes are private, so they're left out unless you tick Include QSO notes.

To check importing and exporting without changing your Solid Copy log, run tools/check-import-export.sh (see Checks).

Removing it

Solid Copy keeps its files in three places. To remove it completely:

  1. The app: in Finder, drag Solid Copy from Applications to the Trash.
  2. Your log and settings: in Finder, choose Go > Go to Folder (Shift-Command-G) and paste ~/Library/Containers/io.github.scott-h5.solidcopy. Move that whole folder to the Trash. It holds your logs (Logbook.sqlite, and any other logs), backups, QSL cards, settings, the saved member and country lists, and the radio log if you recorded one. Export your logs first (File > Export…) if you want to keep the contacts, because emptying the Trash can't bring them back.
  3. Passwords: open Keychain Access, search for "Solid Copy", and delete the entries named "Solid Copy: …" (QRZ.com, LoTW, QRZ Logbook, Club Log, eQSL). Those are the logins for the services you've set up.

Your backups aren't in that folder if you chose another one under Settings > Backups, such as a folder in iCloud Drive. Solid Copy doesn't delete them, so remove that folder yourself if you don't want the copies.

Nothing else is left behind: Solid Copy starts no helper programs, and it lets go of the radio's port when it quits.
